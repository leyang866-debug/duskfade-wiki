## Build your movement chain

Link jumps, dashes, grapples, rails, glides, and attacks into one movement chain. The goal is controlled momentum: keep moving when the route is safe, then reset at a clear landing instead of carrying a bad line forward.

## Keep momentum through platforming

During platforming, aim for the next safe surface rather than the longest possible chain. Grapples and rails extend a route, while a glide gives you time to correct your position. If the camera loses the landing, release lock-on and re-center before committing to the next move.

## Fight with space and dodge timing

Lock-on is useful for a single threat, but it can narrow your view in an arena. Keep a retreat lane open, dodge toward the readable safe space, and commit to an attack only after the boss pattern has finished.

## Return with new tools

New movement tools turn previously blocked routes into return-trip targets. Mark the route when you first find it, then come back after the relevant tool is available. Teleporters make these focused collection runs faster than searching every corner on the first visit.

## Read boss fights as platforming tests

Bosses combine combat and platforming, so movement is part of the damage plan. Preserve a route to safety before a combo, use short punish windows, and treat the four difficulty settings as a way to control how quickly you learn each pattern. Master Clockmaker is the highest setting.

## Gameplay FAQ

### What is the main movement idea in Duskfade?

Keep jumps, dashes, grapples, rails, and glides connected when the route is safe, then reset at a clear landing when it is not.

### Should I keep lock-on enabled while platforming?

Use it for a single threat, then release it when the camera or arena needs a wider view.

### When should I revisit blocked routes?

Mark them during the first visit and return after a new movement tool makes the route practical.
